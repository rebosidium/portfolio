Raiffeisen Bank

Case study

Designing a new trading terminal for portfolio managers and traders

Project & role

Raiffeisen Bank was building an in-house trading terminal for more than 500 institutional clients, including asset managers and brokers.

As Lead Product Designer, I spent around six months designing the terminal from scratch. I interviewed portfolio managers and traders, designed the main screens and workflows, and prepared the widget behaviour, interface states and layout rules for development.

The challenge

The bank wanted to reduce its dependence on external vendors and develop its own product. The existing setup combined QUIK with custom integrations. Users spent considerable time switching between portfolios, and access to banking accounts was constrained by the existing integration. Changes could require lengthy work by an external vendor.

Portfolio managers needed a clear view of their clients’ assets and performance. Traders also needed to act on investment decisions and trading instructions. The new terminal had to bring portfolio information, banking accounts and everyday tools into one workspace.

The bank also wanted to introduce AI into its products. For the terminal, I focused on information gathering, portfolio questions and workspace setup, with trading decisions kept with the user.

Research & process

I interviewed around 10 portfolio managers and traders, reviewed their existing workflows in QUIK and observed how they worked with portfolios and orders. Switching between portfolios and accessing banking accounts emerged as important areas to improve.

I used these findings to shape the workflows and discussed the proposed solutions with users through interviews. We also gave participants the same task in QUIK and the new platform to compare the experience.

The AI-assisted scenarios fell into three groups: finding market information, asking questions about a specific portfolio, and setting up the workspace.

The solution

Portfolios, accounts and daily operations

I designed the workspace around the following scenarios:

  • Switch between portfolios and review their value, holdings and financial performance.
  • Explore portfolio performance over different periods and inspect values on the chart.
  • Review cash, stocks, funds, bonds and futures, with search and category views.
  • Access banking accounts, see balances and when they were last updated, and use actions for funding and withdrawals.
  • Choose an account and target portfolio when buying securities.
  • Review trades, dividends, coupons, taxes and transfers, with amounts, fees and statuses.
  • Find an instrument by name or ticker, distinguish trading venues, and open company information or a chart.

Portfolio-aware AI Highlights

I designed a compact panel for facts, news and events relevant to the selected portfolio. Users could mark highlights as useful or not useful. This feedback was intended to shape the content over time—for example, showing more company news or more information about the portfolio’s composition.

The panel was designed to inform users without directing them to buy or sell.

One assistant, three types of tasks

A single search field supported three groups of scenarios:

  • Market information: find securities, explore information about an instrument and ask for comparisons.
  • Portfolio information: ask questions in ordinary language, such as how much dividend income a portfolio received or which holdings each represented less than 1% of its value. Results could include a short answer, a breakdown and an Excel download.
  • Workspace setup: request a widget or create an entirely new board, then adjust it for the task.

A separate suggestion strip adapted to the instrument or query. For Apple, for example, it offered questions about price movements and valuation, similar instruments, and an action to add a card to a thematic board.

Setting up the workspace

The widget flow included a preview, a choice of card layout, and confirmation before adding it to the board. Users could then position the widget where they needed it.

For whole boards, I designed suggested templates and the resulting workspace. Users could rename, duplicate or delete boards, hide widgets and access their data settings.

Text requests worked alongside direct interface controls, giving users both ways to set up their workspace.

Outcomes

  • Estimated around 4× faster workspace setup than in QUIK, based on observing users complete the same task.
  • The terminal design, including the AI-assisted scenarios, was approved and moved into development.
  • I prepared the screen designs, widget behaviour, main interface states and layout rules for the development team.
