Raiffeisen Leasing

Case study

Simplifying the journey from an initial calculation to contract closure

Project & role

Raiffeisen Leasing Online (RLO) was the company’s in-house platform for the full leasing lifecycle. It connected internal teams, business clients and a network of brokers, dealers, suppliers and insurers. Raiffeisen Leasing managed a €600M corporate leasing portfolio.

As Lead Product Designer from 2021 to 2025, I combined hands-on research and product design with design leadership. My work covered the full leasing lifecycle, with an initial focus on simplifying proposal preparation for managers.

The challenge

RLO supported the business, but its workflows were utilitarian and paid little attention to the user experience. Managers gathered deal information, compared offers, calculated terms and prepared proposal documents before uploading them to the platform. Some used standalone spreadsheets, leaving potential deals unrecorded in RLO.

After encountering limitations with vendor solutions, the company chose to build its own ecosystem. The business goal was to scale leasing through a partner network with minimal manual processing. We started with proposal preparation: managers needed a faster way to explore terms with clients and move an agreed option forward.

According to the company’s 2023 presentation, brokers accounted for 70% of new business in its retail leasing segment in Q1 2022.

Research & process

I interviewed managers and specialists involved in deal processing, including insurance and compliance teams, then ran several usability tests with managers to validate hypotheses and refine the workflows. I also reviewed the existing BPMN diagram to understand the current deal workflow.

The key decision was to separate the information needed for an indicative calculation from the requirements for completing an approved deal. An initial calculation needed core details such as the client’s tax ID, asset cost, contract term and advance payment. Insurance and vehicle documents, compliance checks and financing approvals could be collected at later stages.

I restructured data collection around those stages so managers could explore terms with a client before completing the full set of documents and approvals.

The solution

Creating and exploring proposals in RLO

Managers could create a proposal directly in the platform, discuss it with the client and quickly generate alternatives. Once an option was agreed, they could move it into subsequent processing.

The calculation helped clients assess possible terms without presenting them as a binding offer or final financing approval. The client’s tax ID supported scoring to inform the terms the company could offer.

Connecting the full lifecycle

I designed the subsequent journey through document collection, compliance and security checks, insurance, financing approvals and contract preparation.

The workflow continued after signing, through ongoing payments, the final payment, contract closure and asset buyout. Branching scenarios covered asset loss, a change of insurer or lessee, and changes to the legal entity.

Supporting flexible payment schedules

Payment schedules were one of the most complex areas. I designed linked views of deal parameters and an automatically generated, editable schedule, supporting balloon payments, increasing or decreasing payments and seasonal arrangements.

Managers could customise the schedule for a client, with changes reflected in the relevant deal parameters.

Extending self-service to clients and partners

B2B clients could check statuses, upload documents, access contracts and payment schedules, obtain documents for active contracts and receive electronic status notifications.

For dealers and suppliers, I improved existing application, calculation and tracking workflows. Dealers could generate indicative calculations automatically, without waiting for a manager. Their applications followed a separate business process and reached a manager for further processing.

Shared UX patterns connected client and partner experiences. I also contributed to the launch of a new car-leasing platform with the partner network.

Outcomes

  • Managers’ proposal preparation time fell from 12 to 2 minutes.
  • Dealers could receive indicative calculations within minutes, compared with waits of up to a day when manual processing was required.
  • Managers used RLO more frequently and moved away from standalone spreadsheets.
  • The wider product effort expanded automation across the leasing lifecycle, from proposal preparation through contract servicing and closure.
  • NPS improved, with feedback from managers and clients analysed separately.

Team feedback

Behind the scenes

Design leadership

Alongside hands-on leasing work, I led a group of designers across CIB product teams. I supported hiring, ran design reviews and one-on-ones, and mentored designers on complex scenarios, design-system consistency, professional development and public speaking.

Ways of working

I helped designers apply an adapted Triple Diamond process and contributed to the design roadmap and collaboration with business stakeholders.

Across the designers I supported in CIB, deeper problem exploration, hypothesis testing and earlier discussion with the business helped reduce rework.

Designing LaaS from scratch

I also designed LaaS (Leasing as a Service), a B2B leasing marketplace, from the ground up. The platform was intended to simplify collaboration between brokers and leasing companies.

AI-assisted offer analysis

I developed an AI-assisted system to help managers compare supplier, insurer and competing leasing-company offers. It supported their analysis of suitable options and highlighted opportunities to offer clients more favourable terms.
